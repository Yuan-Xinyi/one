\begin{abstract}
This work studies \textit{extreme motion generation}, which aims to maximize the Cartesian path length along a prescribed path within the manipulator's workspace. While conventional reachability analysis indicates whether each point of a path is attainable, it does not indicate how far a \emph{single continuous joint trajectory} can carry the tool before a joint limit, an orientation tolerance, or a collision terminates the motion---and the two quantities differ substantially. We quantify the realizable part as \textit{continuous kinematic reachability} and measure it per task with a verified two-sided bracket: a pointwise-reachable length, obtained by marching along each task's own path, serves as an upper reference, and the executed stroke is reported as the fraction of it that one continuous motion realizes. \new{Realizing that fraction requires long-horizon judgment delivered at fine-grained action precision, and the central finding of this paper is that the two should be supplied by different instruments: a learned scalar value field carries the long-horizon feasibility, while exact kinematics resolves the local null-space action online. Cast as a discounted exit-time optimal control problem, stroke maximization reduces without loss to switching among the vertices of the null-space command box, governed by a scalar field of the configuration; every controller in this paper is an exact one-step model differentiating such a field, and performance rises with how much future the field carries. A training-free constraint-margin field already matches receding-horizon optimization of its own objective; the critic of an off-the-shelf reinforcement-learning run lifts performance further at identical per-step computation on all three manipulators, while the same run's policy head, asked to output actions directly, stays far below its own critic consumed this way. The remaining decision --- which branch of the task-feasible configuration set to start from --- is a coarse branch-level judgment, delegated to a learned selector supervised for free by rollouts of the deployed law.} We evaluate the framework across three manipulators on 10,000 path-following simulation tasks each, with real-world validations, and show that the same measurement provides a task-aware reference for manipulator base placement. The project website and related videos of this paper can be found at \url{https://yuan-xinyi.github.io/extreme-motion-generation/}.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}




\IEEEPARstart{C}{onsider} a compact, fixed-base manipulator executing a coating or welding operation over a large work surface. Intuition suggests the farthest reachable point is set by the reachable workspace; in practice the motion is continuous, and one of the safety constraints --- joint limits, tool-orientation tolerance, collision --- is violated long before the end-effector approaches the workspace boundary. The achievable path length is therefore decided by how long those boundaries can be deferred during execution. We call the problem of maximizing the Cartesian path length along a prescribed path \textit{extreme motion generation}. It matters wherever the length of a single continuous motion limits the area one setup can process: exploiting the extreme motion enlarges the effective working range without scaling up the manipulator or relocating its base.

(a) Farthest distance with pointwise-valid configurations; (b) farthest distance achievable by a single continuous joint trajectory. \new{The outcome of an extended stroke is decided by two coupled choices no reachability map sees: once, which branch of the task-feasible configuration set the stroke starts on --- a discrete choice no continuous motion can revise --- and at every control step, how the redundancy is resolved, since the joint motion chosen now steers which constraint the arm meets tens of increments later. The horizon itself --- when the stroke ends --- is the quantity optimized, and its natural formal home is a discounted \emph{exit-time} optimal control problem, on which this paper builds directly.}

\new{The exit-time formulation pays off three times. First, its HJB equation is affine in the box-bounded null-space command, so optimal redundancy resolution reduces, without loss, to switching among the $2^{m}$ vertices of the command box, governed by the sign pattern of $\mathbf{G}^{\!\top}\nabla V$: every controller in this paper is an exact one-step model differentiating a scalar potential, and they differ only in what the potential knows about the future. Second, the structure identifies what the classical law gets wrong --- it reads a hand-weighted instantaneous surrogate --- and substituting the analytic \emph{constraint-margin field} for the intractable $V$ yields a training-free law that already matches receding-horizon optimization of its own objective. Third, and centrally, the structure prescribes how learned long-horizon knowledge should be consumed. A reinforcement-learning run trains a policy head and a critic on one experience. Deployed directly, the policy must compress the value gradient into its weights, and it stays below the training-free law; consumed as the scalar field of the same one-step law, the \emph{critic} of the identical run tops every rung at identical per-step computation --- the exact model separates the candidates one step apart, and the smooth learned value ranks the successors, its correlated errors cancelling in the difference. Learning supplies what networks represent well, smooth state values; the model supplies the fine local distinctions they do not. The remaining decision, the branch to start from, is the coarse counterpart of the same division, made by a permutation-equivariant selector from labels that rollouts of the deployed law generate for free.}

\new{We evaluate on path-following tasks across three manipulators, against the pointwise-reachable length of each task, so the gap between pointwise and continuous reachability is itself quantified. The controller ladder orders exactly by how much future the scalar field carries and replicates on all three manipulators; ablations close the loop on the mechanism (horizon pays only on the learned field; the gain requires a value trained toward optimal continuations; the critic sits at the fixed point of policy improvement); and the selector transfers to serpentine and non-planar families it has never seen.}

\new{An earlier version of this work, restricted to one manipulator with a generative initial-configuration model, a learned trajectory stage, and a best-of-$K$ reference, is available as a preprint~\cite{yuan2026extreme}; this article differs in the measurement, in the trajectory stage (derived rather than trained), in the candidate layer, and in scope.}

\new{The contributions are as follows.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \new{\textbf{An exit-time optimal-control characterization, and the family of resolution laws it yields.} Stroke maximization is a discounted exit-time problem whose HJB Hamiltonian is affine in the box-bounded null-space command: the greedy policy with respect to any differentiable value estimate can be taken on the $2^{m}$ vertices, every deployable controller is an exact one-step model differentiating a scalar potential, and the classical law is absorbed into the family. The analytic constraint-margin field yields a training-free law that matches receding-horizon optimization of its own objective and transfers across manipulators without retuning.}
\item \new{\textbf{A measured division of labor between learning and the exact model, and the controller it yields.} One reinforcement-learning run supplies a policy head and a critic. The policy, deployed directly, stays below the training-free margin law; the critic, consumed as the scalar field of the one-step vertex law, surpasses every rung at identical per-step computation on all three morphologies, with no additional training. Ablations attribute the gain to the value itself; optimization horizon pays only on the learned field.}
\item \new{\textbf{A verified two-sided bracket of continuous kinematic reachability.} A best-of-$K$ reference is not sound --- achieved strokes exceed it. We march along each task's own path and test whether any admissible configuration exists per sample; between this pointwise-reachable length and the executed stroke, the pointwise-versus-continuous gap is quantified per task, across three manipulators and over a distribution of tasks, and the same cheap query corrects a systematic optimism in reachability-map-based placement.}
\item \new{\textbf{A selector for the one decision a resolution law cannot make, with supervision that costs nothing.} The branch the stroke starts on is a discrete choice no continuous resolution can revise --- the coarse end of the same division. Every candidate is rolled out under the deployed law, the outcomes are the labels, and a permutation-equivariant selector conditioned on a sampled table of the path ahead chooses in one forward pass, transferring to families it has never seen.}
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Local Dexterity, Reachability, and Global Resolution}
Manipulability characterizes local dexterity through the
Jacobian~\cite{yoshikawa1985manipulability,yoshikawa1985dynamic,chiu1988task,klein1987dexterity}
and has been folded into null-space
resolution~\cite{liegeois1977automatic,jin2017manipulability,jaquier2021geometry,haviland2021neo}
and planning~\cite{shen2023adaptive,cai2025just}; workspace reachability
characterizes global feasibility through capability
maps~\cite{yang2009placement,zacharias2007capturing} used for base
placement~\cite{makhal2018reuleaux,vahrenkamp2013robot} and learned
priors~\cite{feng2025predictive,jauhri2022robot,selim2022safe}. Neither
indicates how joint motion should be coordinated so that a single
continuous path approaches the reachable boundary. Closer to that
question, the global structure of the solution set is well understood:
redundant IK solutions form disjoint self-motion
manifolds~\cite{burdick1989inverse}, joint limits split them into
disconnected branches~\cite{shimizu2008analytical,luck1993self}, and
global resolution has been posed as continuous
pseudoinversion~\cite{hauser2018global} or dynamic programming across
homotopy classes~\cite{ferrentino2020optimal,ferrentino2021globally,fabregat2025topological};
extremal solutions are globally optimal within their own
class~\cite{martin1989resolution,suh1987local,kazerounian1988global}.
These works treat the Cartesian path as given and ask how to execute it
optimally --- not how far it can be extended, nor how much of the
achievable extent is decided by the branch as against the law applied
within it, measured over a distribution of tasks. Learning-based
resolution~\cite{malik2022deep} and generative IK
solvers~\cite{ames2022ikflow,zhang2025ikdiffuser,huang2024diffusionseeder,yoon2023learning}
model the multiplicity of solutions but are likewise evaluated on how
well a prescribed path is executed. This paper draws the line between
learning and analysis by measurement: learning for state-level judgments
(branch selection, successor evaluation), the exact model for
action-level distinctions, with learned reactive policies evaluated as
baselines under identical conditions.

\subsection{\new{Quantifying Continuous Reachability}}
\new{Two lines of work are close enough that the differences must be
stated precisely. The first asks whether a prescribed path can be
executed at all by one continuous joint trajectory: the hierarchy of
pointwise, pathwise and global resolution~\cite{hauser2016continuous},
paths whose waypoints are individually reachable while no continuous
resolution exists~\cite{hauser2018global}, and the $t$-connected regions
of the cuspidal literature~\cite{wenger1991uniqueness,wenger2023cuspidal,elias2025cuspidal}
--- set-valued or binary statements: a region either has the property or
it does not. The second line counts interruptions when a path cannot be
covered entirely, minimizing reconfigurations or
breakpoints~\cite{wang2024iklink,yang2022optimal,yin2024breakpoints}; a
count yields no arc length and no upper reference, and by permitting
reconfiguration cannot ask how far a \emph{single} stroke reaches.
Closest in spirit, Gautier et al.~\cite{gautier2024comparison} report
maximum weldable extents along prescribed trajectories, but as
case-study numbers for a non-redundant arm, one-sided by construction.
What is missing --- and what this paper supplies --- is a per-task,
two-sided, distribution-level measurement, and the attribution of the
measured extent to the choices that produce it.}

\section{Problem Formulation}
While extreme motion generation applies broadly to constrained path following, we here specify it as extreme straight-line path following. Specifically, a fixed-base manipulator advances its end-effector along the line with a tool-orientation constraint. Solving it requires choosing the initial joint configuration and then generating the motion that maximizes the achievable path length.



Formally, consider a manipulator with $n$ degrees of freedom, where $\mathbf{q} \in \mathbb{R}^{n}$ denotes the joint configuration. Let $\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{q}) \in \mathbb{R}^{3}$ denote the end-effector position and $\mathbf{z}(\mathbf{q}) \in \mathbb{R}^{3}$ denote the TCP local $z$-axis. A straight-line path-following task is specified by the condition vector $\mathbf{c} = [\mathbf{p}_{0}, \mathbf{d}, \mathbf{n}] \in \mathbb{R}^{9}$, where $\mathbf{p}_{0}$ is the starting position and $\mathbf{d}$, $\mathbf{n}$ are unit vectors giving the motion direction and task-plane normal. \new{The prescribed path is the ray $\mathcal{P}=\{\mathbf{p}_{0}+s\,\mathbf{d} : s\ge 0\}$, along which the end-effector advances at a commanded speed $v$, one command being issued per control period $\Delta t$.} \new{The straight ray is the default member of a family sharing this condition vector: a signed curvature bends it into an arc, a sinusoidal lateral offset turns it into a serpentine seam, and a rotating cone axis $\mathbf{n}(s)$ models a non-planar workpiece. All four families are evaluated in Section~\ref{sec:setup}; the formulation carries over with $\mathbf{d}$ read as the instantaneous tangent and $\mathbf{n}$ as the local cone axis.}

Starting from an initial configuration $\mathbf{q}_{0}^{*}$, the policy $\pi$ chooses the joint motion at every control step, producing a joint trajectory that ends at step $T$, when a safety constraint is first violated. \new{The executed motion is a \emph{stroke} of Cartesian length $\mathcal{L}$.} The task is the constrained optimization problem:
\begin{align}
\max_{\mathbf{q}_{0}^{*},\,\pi} \quad & \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{q}^{*}_{0},\,\pi;\,\mathbf{p}_{0}, \mathbf{d}, \mathbf{n}) \\
\text{s.t.} \quad & {\color{red}\operatorname{dist}\bigl(\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{q}_{t}^{*}),\,\mathcal{P}\bigr) \leq \epsilon_{\text{pos}}}, \label{eq:pos_constraint} \\
& \arccos\!\left( \mathbf{z}(\mathbf{q}_{t}^{*})^{\top} \mathbf{n} \right) \leq \theta_{\max}, \label{eq:ori_constraint} \\
& \mathbf{q}_{\min} \preceq \mathbf{q}_{t}^{*} \preceq \mathbf{q}_{\max}, \label{eq:jl_constraint}
\end{align}
where $\mathcal{L}$ denotes the stroke length, $\mathbf{q}_0^*$ and $\pi$ are the initial configuration and the control policy to be optimized, and $(\mathbf{p}_0, \mathbf{d}, \mathbf{n})$ specify the task conditions as above. The $\epsilon_{\text{pos}}$, $\theta_{\max}$, and $[\mathbf{q}_{\min}, \mathbf{q}_{\max}]$ are the position tolerance, orientation tolerance, and joint limits, respectively.

Since only the $3$-DoF position must be tracked, the manipulator is kinematically redundant for $n>3$, and the commanded joint velocity decomposes into a task term and a null-space term,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:nullspace}
{\color{red}
\dot{\mathbf{q}} = \mathbf{J}^{+}_{\lambda}\dot{\mathbf{x}} + \mathbf{B}(\mathbf{q})\,\mathbf{u},
\qquad
\mathbf{J}\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{0},\quad
\mathbf{B}^{\!\top}\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{I},}
\end{equation}
\new{where $\mathbf{J}^{+}_{\lambda}$ is the damped pseudo-inverse of the
position Jacobian and $\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{q})$ an orthonormal basis of its
\emph{exact} null space (double-precision SVD; task-aligned construction in
Section~\ref{sec:traj}), so $\mathbf{J}\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{0}$ holds by
construction and the secondary motion cannot disturb the end-effector
regardless of the damping; $\mathbf{u}$ collects the null-space coordinates.}
Within this null space the classical law performs gradient ascent on a
weighted secondary field,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:secondary}
\boldsymbol{\xi}=k_{\mu}\,\nabla_{\mathbf{q}}w_{\mathbf{d}}(\mathbf{q})\;-\;k_{\mathrm{jl}}\,\frac{\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{mid}}}{\mathbf{q}_{\max}-\mathbf{q}_{\min}}\;+\;k_{\theta}\,g(\theta)\,\nabla_{\mathbf{q}}\!\big(\mathbf{z}(\mathbf{q})^{\top}\mathbf{n}\big),
\end{equation}
where the secondary field $\boldsymbol{\xi}$ combines a directional-manipulability term $w_{\mathbf{d}}$ that retreats from singularities along $\mathbf{d}$, a centering term that pulls each joint toward the middle $\mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{mid}}$ of its range, and a cone term gated by $g(\theta)$ that activates only as the tool axis approaches the $\theta_{\max}$ boundary. \new{It enters the decomposition as $\mathbf{u}\propto\mathbf{B}^{\!\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}$; all controllers share Eq.~(\ref{eq:nullspace}) and differ only in how $\mathbf{u}$ is generated.}

{\color{red}
The horizon of this problem is not an input: the stroke ends at the first constraint violation, and when that happens is itself decided by the commands being optimized. The natural formal home for such an objective is an \emph{exit-time} optimal control problem, stated here and analysed in Section~\ref{sec:hjb}.

Collect the constraints of
Eqs.~(\ref{eq:pos_constraint})--(\ref{eq:jl_constraint}), together with the
self-collision clearance, into an open feasible set $\Omega$ of configurations,
and let $\tau = \inf\{t \ge 0 : \mathbf{q}(t) \notin \Omega\}$ denote the exit time of a closed-loop trajectory (not to be confused with the switching thresholds of Section~\ref{sec:traj}). Throughout this work the null-space coordinates
are bounded, $\mathbf{u} = a_{\max}\,\mathbf{a}$ with
$\mathbf{a} \in \mathcal{A} := [-1,1]^{m}$, where $m = n-3$ is the dimension
of the null space left by the position task ($m=4$ for the two 7-DoF arms,
$m=3$ for Cobotta). Under this bound the decomposition of
Eq.~(\ref{eq:nullspace}) is control-affine,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:affine}
\dot{\mathbf{q}} \;=\; \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{q}) \;+\; \mathbf{G}(\mathbf{q})\,\mathbf{a},
\qquad
\mathbf{f} := \mathbf{J}^{+}_{\lambda}\dot{\mathbf{x}},\quad
\mathbf{G} := a_{\max}\,\mathbf{B},
\end{equation}
with the task condition $\mathbf{c}$ entering $\mathbf{f}$ and $\Omega$ as a
fixed parameter. Writing $r(\mathbf{q}) \ge 0$ for the instantaneous advance
rate along $\mathbf{d}$ --- the continuous-time counterpart of the reward
defined in Section~\ref{sec:traj} --- define the discounted progress value
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:value}
V(\mathbf{q}_{0}) \;=\; \sup_{\mathbf{a}(\cdot)}
\int_{0}^{\tau} e^{-\beta t}\, r(\mathbf{q}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t,
\end{equation}
where the supremum runs over measurable commands into $\mathcal{A}$ and $\beta>0$ matches the implementation's discount. On the straight-line family the task term holds the advance rate at $v$, so $V$ increases with the exit time alone: the discounted problem is a maximum-exit-time problem. $V$ in (\ref{eq:value}) is the optimal value; the learned components of this paper approximate values of \emph{policies}. The two decision variables reappear as the two stages: selection chooses $\mathbf{q}_{0}^{*}$ across the disconnected components of the feasible set, and the trajectory stage resolves the redundancy at every increment toward the same objective.

Two facts, both properties of the system as constructed, are all the analysis of Section~\ref{sec:hjb} uses: the dynamics (\ref{eq:affine}) are affine in $\mathbf{a}$, and the advance rate $r$ does not depend on $\mathbf{a}$ because $\mathbf{J}\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{0}$ holds exactly. What separates the exit-time form from instantaneous null-space optimization is the argument of the maximization alone: a command at $\mathbf{q}$ is judged by how it changes all future feasible progress, through $V$, not by any quantity evaluable at $\mathbf{q}$ in isolation.
}

\section{\texorpdfstring{\new{HJB Structure and Vertex-Valued Feedback}}{HJB Structure and Vertex-Valued Feedback}}
\label{sec:hjb}

{\color{red}
This section derives what the exit-time formulation implies about the optimal secondary command: the command box can be replaced by its $2^{m}$ vertices without loss; the classical law belongs to the same family as the optimal one, differing in two identifiable substitutions; and reversing the substitutions with deployable scalar fields yields the resolution laws of this paper.

\subsection{HJB Characterization}
At points of differentiability the value function (\ref{eq:value}) satisfies the HJB relation of the discounted exit-time problem,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hjb}
\beta V \;=\; r \;+\; \nabla V^{\!\top}\mathbf{f}
\;+\; \sup_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{A}}
\bigl(\mathbf{G}^{\!\top}\nabla V\bigr)^{\!\top}\mathbf{a},
\qquad V \equiv 0 \ \text{outside } \Omega .
\end{equation}
Because $r$ and $\mathbf{f}$ do not depend on $\mathbf{a}$, the command enters
only through the last term, linearly. Define the \emph{switching vector}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigma}
\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\mathbf{q}) \;:=\;
\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{q})^{\!\top}\nabla V(\mathbf{q}) \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{m},
\end{equation}
whose $i$-th component is the marginal effect of the $i$-th null-space direction on discounted future feasible progress. The supremum in (\ref{eq:hjb}) equals $\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}\|_{1}$ and, wherever no component vanishes, is attained at the single vertex $\mathbf{a} = \operatorname{sgn}\boldsymbol{\sigma}$: the resolution implied by the value function selects an orthant, not a magnitude.

\subsection{Lossless Vertex Reduction}
The observation above does not require $V$ to be the optimal value function;
it holds for the gradient field of any scalar function of the configuration,
which is what makes it usable by a learner whose value estimate is never
exact.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:vertex}
Let $W:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}$ be differentiable and
$\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{W} := \mathbf{G}^{\!\top}\nabla W$. Then, at every
$\mathbf{q}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:prop}
\max_{\mathbf{a}\in[-1,1]^{m}} \boldsymbol{\sigma}_{W}^{\!\top}\mathbf{a}
\;=\;
\max_{\mathbf{a}\in\{-1,1\}^{m}} \boldsymbol{\sigma}_{W}^{\!\top}\mathbf{a}
\;=\; \|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{W}\|_{1},
\end{equation}
attained at $\mathbf{a}=\operatorname{sgn}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{W}$, with the
components on which $\sigma_{W,i}=0$ chosen arbitrarily in $\{-1,1\}$.
Consequently the greedy policy with respect to any differentiable value
estimate can be taken vertex-valued: the vertex action space is closed under
policy improvement.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
$\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{W}^{\!\top}\mathbf{a}
= \sum_{i}\sigma_{W,i}\,a_{i} \le \sum_{i}|\sigma_{W,i}|$ for
$\mathbf{a}\in[-1,1]^{m}$, with equality iff
$a_{i}=\operatorname{sgn}\sigma_{W,i}$ on every component with
$\sigma_{W,i}\neq 0$; such an $\mathbf{a}$ can be chosen in $\{-1,1\}^{m}$.
\end{IEEEproof}

\begin{remark}[Scope]
\label{rem:bangbang}
Three delimitations. The box- and vertex-constrained problems share their pointwise Hamiltonian by (\ref{eq:prop}); equality of the value functions would need viscosity comparison conditions we neither verify nor use. Where components of $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$ vanish, an optimal vertex-valued \emph{selector exists} rather than every optimal command being a vertex. And the reduction is exact in continuous time only; the finite-rate gap is answered empirically in Section~\ref{sec:setup}, and the box itself is a design choice in the constructed basis with no canonical status.
\end{remark}



The structural conclusion is the kinematic counterpart of a classical mechanism: in time-optimal control along a prescribed path, the torque box with control-affine dynamics forces the optimal acceleration to the boundary of the admissible set except on singular arcs~\cite{bobrow1985time,shin1985minimum}.

\subsection{From the Classical Law to the Analytic Margin Law}
\label{sec:relation}
The classical secondary command of Eq.~(\ref{eq:secondary}) performs gradient
ascent on a weighted instantaneous objective; writing that objective, gains
and $a_{\max}$ absorbed, as $\Phi(\mathbf{q})$, the two laws read, in the
shared basis and up to scaling,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cl-vs-opt}
\mathbf{a}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\mathbf{q})
= \operatorname{clip}\bigl(\mathbf{G}^{\!\top}\nabla\Phi(\mathbf{q})\bigr),
\qquad
\mathbf{a}^{*}(\mathbf{q})
= \operatorname{sgn}\bigl(\mathbf{G}^{\!\top}\nabla V(\mathbf{q})\bigr).
\end{equation}
They differ in exactly two substitutions: the scalar field ($\Phi$ a hand-designed surrogate at the current configuration, $V$ the discounted future feasible progress) and the use of the gradient (the classical law keeps its magnitude, so the command shrinks where the surrogate is flat; the value-based feedback keeps only its sign). The two laws share one feedback form, $\mathbf{a} = h\bigl(\mathbf{G}^{\!\top}\nabla(\cdot)\bigr)$.

$V$ itself has no closed form and its domain --- configurations crossed with task conditions --- is far beyond grid-based HJB solvers. But the exit-time structure says what the value gradient defends: on this family $V$ increases with the exit time alone, and the exit is caused by the first margin to reach zero. This motivates replacing $V$ by the \emph{constraint-margin field}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:margin-field}
\Phi_{\mathrm{m}}(\mathbf{q})
= -\tau_{\mathrm{m}} \log \sum_{j} \exp\bigl(-m_{j}(\mathbf{q})/\tau_{\mathrm{m}}\bigr),
\end{equation}
a soft minimum over the normalized joint-limit and orientation-cone margins
$m_{j}$ --- the constraints whose exhaustion ends strokes. The deployed
training-free law is the vertex law of Proposition~\ref{prop:vertex} read
on this field,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:margin-law}
\mathbf{a}_{\mathrm{m}}(\mathbf{q})
= \operatorname{sgn}\bigl(\mathbf{G}^{\!\top}\nabla\Phi_{\mathrm{m}}(\mathbf{q})\bigr),
\end{equation}
optionally in its finite-rate form: the $2^{m}$ vertices scored by
$\Phi_{\mathrm{m}}$ one held command ahead, exact for the control period
that Remark~\ref{rem:bangbang} prices. Relative to the classical law it
reverses both substitutions of (\ref{eq:cl-vs-opt}): the field measures
proximity to \emph{all} exits jointly, and the sign keeps full authority
where the field is flat. It has no trained parameters and no
per-manipulator gains.

\subsection{From the Margin Field to a Learned Value Field}
\label{sec:vlook}
The margin field reads the \emph{present}; the field that measures which configurations keep exits far for the longest time is the value function itself, and an estimate already exists wherever a reinforcement-learning baseline has been trained: the critic of an actor--critic run regresses precisely the discounted future progress of Eq.~(\ref{eq:value}) under its own improving policy. Reading that critic $\widehat{V}$ through the same finite-rate law gives the third member of the family,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:value-law}
\mathbf{a}_{V}(\mathbf{q})
= \arg\max_{\mathbf{a}\in\{-1,1\}^{m},\; F(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{a})\in\Omega}
\widehat{V}\bigl(F(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{a})\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $F(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{a})$ is the exact one-step flow of
Eq.~(\ref{eq:affine}) under the held command and the feasibility test
discards vertices whose successor leaves $\Omega$. This is the finite-rate
finite-difference reading of $\operatorname{sgn}(\mathbf{G}^{\!\top}\nabla
\widehat{V})$, and it is also one exact step of policy improvement, computed
at deployment time instead of by further training: the policy greedy with
respect to a policy's value is guaranteed not to be worse than that policy.

Two properties make this consumption of a learned value reliable where a learned \emph{policy} is not. First, the quantity a direct policy must represent is the gradient sign of $V$ --- by Eq.~(\ref{eq:cl-vs-opt}) the optimal action \emph{is} a derivative of the value --- and approximating a function well does not bound the error of its derivative; the training signal compounds the asymmetry, because the critic receives a regression target at every state while the policy gradient must extract the best-versus-second-best difference from returns whose variance exceeds it by orders of magnitude. Second, Eq.~(\ref{eq:value-law}) never asks the network for a derivative: the exact model converts the action discrimination into a comparison of $2^{m}$ distinct states, and because the approximation error of a smooth network is itself smooth, the errors at neighbouring successors largely cancel in the comparison. The network stores the smooth long-horizon potential; the kinematic model performs the differentiation without error. Section~\ref{sec:setup} tests every link of this argument.
}

\section{Extreme Motion Generation}
\new{This section specifies the two stages in turn.} \new{Before execution ($t=0$), a candidate layer enumerates initial configurations for the task condition $\mathbf{c}$ and a learned selector retains one of them as $\mathbf{q}_{0}^{*}$. During execution ($t>0$), the joint trajectory is generated step by step, resolving the redundancy at every increment with the one-step vertex law of Section~\ref{sec:hjb} --- reading the margin field of Eq.~(\ref{eq:margin-law}) when no training is available, and the learned value field of Eq.~(\ref{eq:value-law}) when it is. Both stages instantiate one division of labor, justified quantitatively in Section~\ref{sec:division}: learning is asked only for smooth, state-level judgments --- which branch is worth starting from, how promising a successor configuration is --- while the fine distinctions between actions are computed by the exact model at decision time.}





\subsection{Initial Joint Configuration Selection}
\label{sec:select}
% quick state that the smm shows that the infinte joint configurations are actually in disconnected branchs. so this module is to ensure that the derived framework doesn't start from an invalid initalizing joint angle.(detailed analysis of the smm put in the appendix). Then introduce the generation process.

For many tasks the achievable path length is largely determined before motion begins: a fixed end-effector pose leaves a one-dimensional self-motion manifold, and joint limits split it into disconnected branches whose configurations share the pose yet differ sharply in reachable path length. \new{This structure, and its consequence for redundancy resolution, are long established: global resolution decomposes into an outer choice among self-motion manifolds and an inner optimization within one, null-space techniques cannot leave the manifold they start on~\cite{burdick1989characterization}, and joint limits split the manifold into disconnected branches~\cite{luck1993self,shimizu2008analytical}. One precision matters here: the task pins only the tool position and constrains the orientation by an inequality, so the candidates span the connected components of the task-feasible configuration set at the starting point, of which fixed-pose self-motion-manifold branches are the fully constrained special case. We keep the shorter word \emph{branch} throughout. What this paper adds is not the structure but a measurement of its magnitude, in the sense made precise in Section~\ref{sec:setup}.}



Therefore, this selection module aims to provide \new{the trajectory generation stage} with an initial joint configuration within a favorable branch. \new{The candidate layer must supply coverage of the branches, not precision: the IKSel table~\cite{yuan2026iksel} is queried along $N_{\mathrm{dir}}$ cone directions, reranked by the full Jacobian metric, refined, and reduced to $K$ candidates by farthest-point selection. The achieved length saturates in branches reached and is insensitive to per-candidate refinement.}

\new{Which candidate to start from cannot be read off any instantaneous geometric quantity --- the consequence becomes visible only at the end of the stroke --- so the selector is supervised by the outcome itself: every candidate of every training task is rolled out under the deployed law and the stroke lengths are the targets. Relabeling for a new manipulator, path family or deployed law is a batch rollout.}

\new{The score is permutation-equivariant over the candidate set (each candidate encoded with a mean-pooled context of the others, listwise ranking loss), and conditioned on the task as a \emph{road table} --- the path ahead sampled at fixed arc lengths --- which is what lets one selector serve every family of Section~III. Deployment costs one table query, one forward pass and one refinement; no candidate is rolled out.}

\subsection{Task-Constrained Joint Trajectory Generation}
\label{sec:traj}
\new{The stage generates the trajectory incrementally --- neither the horizon nor the number of decision variables is known before the problem is solved, since the quantity maximized is the length at which the trajectory ends. At each increment the end-effector advances a fixed arc length (imposing Eq.~\eqref{eq:pos_constraint} increment by increment, so deviation never accumulates) and the redundancy is resolved in the null space of the position Jacobian; the only design choice is the null-space command $\mathbf{u}$.}



\new{We therefore replace what the command reads, not the feedback form. At
each increment the $2^{m}$ vertices of the command box are advanced one held
command ahead by the exact model, infeasible successors are discarded, and
the survivors are scored by a scalar field; the maximizer is executed. Two
fields are deployed. The \emph{margin field} $\Phi_{\mathrm{m}}$ of
Eq.~(\ref{eq:margin-law}) is training-free: reading a soft \emph{minimum}
of all margins re-chooses the balance among the competing constraints at
every increment instead of fixing it by one set of weights, and reading the
field by sign keeps the command at full authority even where the margins
are locally flat. The \emph{learned value field} of
Eq.~(\ref{eq:value-law}) additionally carries the long-horizon consequences
the margins cannot see --- a configuration may stand far from every limit
and still be tens of increments from an unavoidable exit --- and comes for
free from the critic of the reinforcement-learning baseline below, with no
training beyond it. Both laws cost the same $2^{m}$ successor evaluations
per increment. On the curved members of the path family the task term
additionally carries a lateral feedback that holds the tool inside the
tolerance tube of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pos_constraint}; it is inert on straight
rays.}

Every resolution law outputs an $m$-dimensional command $\mathbf{a}\in[-1,1]^{m}$ in a task-aligned null-space basis $\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{q})$, constructed by Gram--Schmidt inside the exact null space of the position Jacobian (double-precision SVD, so the null-space command cannot leak into the task motion): the three projected objective gradients of Eq.~\eqref{eq:secondary} are orthonormalized first and the orthogonal complement completes the basis. The command $\dot{\mathbf{q}}_{\mathrm{null}}=\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{q})\,(a_{\max}\mathbf{a})$ is added to the same task term for every law, so all laws \new{share one parameterization and are interchangeable at any increment}.

\new{\paragraph*{Learned baseline policies} The experiments compare the deployed laws against policies trained with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) under identical conditions, over the continuous box and over its $2^{m}$ vertices with a categorical head, observing the quantities needed to resolve the redundancy:}
Their observation exposes the normalized joint configuration and its square (making proximity to either limit directly visible), the task vectors $\mathbf{d}$ and $\mathbf{n}$, the tool axis with its cross and inner products with $\mathbf{n}$ (the orientation error relative to the cone), and the previous command; the end-effector position is excluded because deviation from the path is held inside the tolerance by the task term. \new{Their reward is the clipped per-step progress along $\mathbf{d}$, normalized by $v\,\Delta t$, with no terminal penalty, so the return approximates the survival time and the trained policy pursues exactly the objective of Eq.~(\ref{eq:value})}. \new{A supervised variant of the vertex policy hands the increment to the classical law inside a thin boundary shell on the normalized joint-limit distance, with hysteresis $(0.98,0.94)$ fixed by a sweep; because all laws share the basis and task term, the end-effector increment is unchanged at a switch.}

\subsection{\new{Where Learning Has Resolution}}
\label{sec:division}
\new{The architecture assigns work by what each instrument resolves. Learning resolves \emph{states}: a value network regressing stroke outcomes reaches $R^{2}$ above $0.9$, and candidate starts spread by hundreds of millimeters. Learning does not resolve \emph{actions}: the $2^{m}$ vertices differ in one-step margin by $5\times10^{-3}$ to $2\times10^{-2}$, direct best-vertex classification attains $24.7\%$ held-out accuracy, and seven probe parameterizations --- $\gamma$-swept and margin-shaped PPO ($1.37$--$1.45$), fitted value iteration ($1.12$--$1.25$), one-step argmax classification ($0.93$) --- never beat the free analytic gradient. The exact model closes exactly this gap, converting the action discrimination into a comparison of distinct states; the system therefore places learned components only in state-scoring positions, and the actor--critic decomposition of Section~\ref{sec:ladder} shows both halves inside one training run.}

\section{Experiments and Analysis}
\label{sec:setup}
\new{All experiments use the \textit{One} robotic system\footnote{https://github.com/wanweiwei07/one} on the 7-DoF xArm7, the 7-DoF Franka Research 3 (FR3) and the 6-DoF Cobotta, with a pen-shaped tool; the same candidate layer, selector and resolution laws run on all three, only the kinematic model changes. Shared settings: cone half-angle $30^{\circ}$, $K=8$ candidates from $N_{\mathrm{dir}}=32$ directions, $\tau_{\mathrm{m}}=0.1$, classical weights $(0.8,0.4,0.2)$, supervisor thresholds $(0.98,0.94)$.} The evaluation set \new{for each manipulator} comprises $10{,}000$ tasks, each specified by a starting position $\mathbf{p}_0$, a desired line direction $\mathbf{d}$, and a task-plane normal $\mathbf{n}$, sampled \new{within that manipulator's own work range, so the three sets are not in correspondence and only the realized fraction is comparable across them}. \new{The evaluation tasks are disjoint from those used to train the selector and the policy.} 

\new{Unless otherwise specified, experiments use the default setting: (1) a candidate pool of $K$ configurations retrieved from the IKSel table along $N_{\mathrm{dir}}$ directions inside the orientation cone, with the task-generating configuration as a fallback slot; (2) a road-table-conditioned selector that picks one candidate, followed by a single damped-least-squares refinement and no deployment-time rollout; (3) the one-step vertex law with temperature $\tau_{\mathrm{m}}=0.1$ for the margin field; the learned value field is the critic of the vertex-PPO baseline of Section~\ref{sec:traj} ($30$M steps, $1.75$\,h wall-clock on one GPU, trained once per manipulator and consumed with no further training). }

\new{A stroke must pass through every point of the path up to where it stops, so the first sample admitting no feasible configuration bounds the achievable length from above: marching along each task's path and searching per sample under all constraints gives the \emph{pointwise-reachable length} $\ell^{\mathrm{pw}}$. The quantity involves no resolution law; its status as an upper reference is checked, not assumed, task by task and under a doubled search budget, as part of the released benchmark.}






\subsection{\new{Evaluation Protocol}}
\new{The controller comparison is paired per task, so its primary statistic is the per-task ratio to the classical law, which needs no reference length; the bracket enters where a per-task scale is needed --- the pointwise-versus-continuous gap of Fig.~\ref{fig:pwgap} and the placement study --- and references that executed strokes exceed (a voxelized reachability chord, the best-of-$K$ rollouts of~\cite{yuan2026extreme}) are excluded by exactly this test.}

\new{Comparisons are paired per task (mean and median, bootstrap $95\%$ intervals); differences within the two-training-run noise floor are reported as indistinguishable.}

\subsubsection{\new{Action-Space Structure}}
\new{The command's shape confirms the vertex reduction empirically. The classical command is an interior point of the box (median magnitude $0.14$ of the half-width); the learned continuous policy saturates it (median $1.00$, $89.2\%$ of steps on the boundary). Projecting the continuous command onto the nearest vertex changes the realized length by $+0.004$ with an interval spanning zero --- the expressible magnitudes carry no information --- while a policy trained natively over the $2^{4}$ vertices exceeds the continuous one by $+0.088$ with intervals excluding zero. The continuous learned resolution is the discrete switching controller of Section~\ref{sec:hjb}, expressed through a parameterization (tanh saturation) that trains worse.}

\subsubsection{\new{Executability and Finite-Rate Consistency}}
\new{A switch alters only the null-space component of the increment, so the end-effector motion is unaffected and the joint-space discontinuity is bounded by $a_{\max}$; generated trajectories are time-parameterized with standard methods~\cite{pham2018toppra} and checked against manufacturer limits before replay.}


\new{Time parameterization repairs the trajectory after the fact; a second
test constrains the command as it is issued. A rate limiter
$|\mathbf{a}_{k}-\mathbf{a}_{k-1}|\le\Delta a_{\max}$ is applied at
deployment, and $\Delta a_{\max}$ is swept from $2$ (no restriction: any
vertex can follow any other) down to $0.125$, reading the trade-off between
the stroke retained and the acceleration percentiles. If the stroke survives moderate rate limiting, the
high-frequency vertex sequence is not itself physically essential --- the
law's benefit lies in which orthant it selects, not in the hardware
executing every switch --- and smoothed deployment is justified.}






\new{Refining the integration step to $25$ and $12.5$\,ms changes absolute lengths by less than $2\%$ and leaves every comparison essentially unchanged, and the projected-versus-continuous paired difference stays positive at $25$ and $50$\,ms control periods, turning negative only at $100$\,ms and beyond --- the sign flipping exactly where the theory predicts intermediate magnitudes begin to substitute for switches the controller can no longer make. The one-step laws are defined at exactly the deployed control period.}

\new{The within-pool oracle costs $K$ rollouts per task; the selector spends one forward pass and gives up the measured remainder --- a trade that matters at scale, where the placement study sweeps thousands of base poses.}

\subsubsection{Manipulator Base Placement Analysis}
\label{sec:place}
\new{Deployment poses the inverse question: the path is given and the base pose must be chosen so it can be executed; waypoint-wise reachability admits poses from which no single stroke covers the path (Fig.~\ref{fig:pwgap}).}

\new{The framework supplies the missing test directly: for each candidate base pose, transform the required path into that base frame, run the two stages, and record the fraction of the required path one continuous stroke covers; a pose is admissible only when the fraction reaches one, and a pose is then chosen inside the admissible set by whatever secondary criterion the cell imposes.}

\new{Two consequences follow. The admissible set obtained this way is contained in the reachability-map set but is not a shrunken copy of it, since what removes a pose is the branch structure rather than distance to the workspace edge. And because the strokes run as far as the kinematics permit, a path that a conservative placement would split into several setups may be covered from a single base pose.}



\subsection{Real-World Validation}
\new{Everything above is kinematic, so a physical trial has to establish executability: that the limit and collision models agree with the hardware and the tool stays on the path once the trajectory is time-parameterized and tracked by the manufacturer's controller. Generated trajectories are replayed unmodified and compared against simulation.}




%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\new{This paper defined and quantified continuous kinematic reachability: how much of a manipulator's pointwise reachability a single continuous stroke can realize, measured per task by a verified two-sided bracket, across three manipulators and over a distribution of tasks.}

\new{The framework rests on one principle, derived from the exit-time formulation and confirmed at every scale: extreme motion generation needs long-horizon judgment delivered at fine action precision, and the two should come from different instruments. The HJB analysis reduces redundancy resolution to differentiating a scalar potential with an exact one-step model; the ladder orders precisely by how much future the potential carries; the critic of an ordinary reinforcement-learning run, read through the same law, tops the ladder on all three manipulators at identical per-step cost, while the policy head of the same run stays below the training-free rung. Learning reliably supplies smooth state values; the exact model converts them into fine action distinctions. The same division decides the start, through a selector supervised for free by rollouts of the deployed law.}

\new{Beyond the benchmark, the same measurement turns base placement into a task-aware decision; extending the bracket to dynamic constraints and the road table to paths specified online are natural next steps.}
