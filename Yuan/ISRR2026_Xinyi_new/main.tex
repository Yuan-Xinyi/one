\rev{Redundant manipulators admit infinitely many joint configurations for a given end-effector pose, and how this redundancy is resolved is decisive for long-horizon tasks such as following a straight-line Cartesian path, where the arm must sustain motion without violating joint limits, leaving the tool-orientation tolerance cone, or crossing kinematic singularities. Learning-based redundancy resolvers capture the multi-modal inverse-kinematics distribution, but because they are trained by density sampling, configurations near the boundary of the feasible set---precisely those that govern when a task terminates---are underrepresented and handled poorly. We present a joint-limit-aware redundancy-resolution framework whose core is a \emph{step-level hybrid controller}: at every control step a hysteresis rule monitors the proximity to the joint limits and delegates boundary regions to a certified classical null-space controller, while granting the unconstrained interior to a reinforcement-learning policy trained to maximize the path-following length. A diffusion-based seed selector complements the controller by initializing the arm on a robust branch of the self-motion manifold. On a held-out set of $10{,}000$ stratified straight-line tasks for a 7-DoF Franka~FR3, evaluated through length ratios against a reference length, the proposed system extends the path-following length by \rev{$1.26\times$} over a classical null-space baseline and recovers \rev{$90.6\%$} of the reference length.}

\section{Introduction}
Straight-line Cartesian path following is a motion primitive shared by a wide range of industrial manipulation tasks, such as arc welding, deburring, and surface inspection. In these tasks, the end-effector must translate along a prescribed line while keeping the tool axis within a tolerance cone about the target surface normal. For a redundant manipulator, the extra degrees of freedom (DoFs) admit an infinite set of joint configurations that realize the same end-effector pose. By exploiting this redundancy to avoid joint limits, the arm can sustain the prescribed motion longer. \rev{The redundancy-resolution strategy therefore determines how far a manipulator of a given size can follow the path.}




\rev{Classical redundancy resolution exploits this freedom through task decomposition: a primary task velocity realizes the desired end-effector motion, while a null-space term optimizes a secondary objective. Such controllers are myopic---they optimize an instantaneous surrogate and overlook its long-horizon consequences---yet near the joint limits their behavior is robust and predictable by design. Learning-based resolvers instead capture the multi-modal IK distribution and can act with foresight; however, because they are trained by density sampling, near-boundary configurations are rarely observed, so a learned policy is least reliable in exactly the region the classical controller handles best. The two paradigms are thus reliable in complementary regions: the learning-based resolver in the well-sampled interior, where foresight is most rewarded, and the classical controller near the boundary, where robustness is guaranteed by construction.}

\rev{Motivated by this observation, we argue that reliability requirements are not uniform over the configuration space, and that a single policy need not be reliable everywhere. We therefore partition control responsibility by proximity to the joint-limit boundary, assigning each region to the paradigm that is reliable within it. In the well-sampled interior, a reinforcement-learning (RL) policy resolves the redundancy to maximize the path-following length. Because it acts while the arm is still far from any limit, it can exploit long-horizon foresight to shape the configuration in advance, precisely where training data are dense and such foresight is reliable. As the arm approaches a joint limit, control is handed to a classical null-space controller, whose graceful and predictable behavior is guaranteed by construction. The two regimes are switched at the level of individual control steps by a hysteresis rule on the normalized distance to the nearest joint limit, which suppresses chattering. In this way, the RL policy is never required to act in the under-sampled boundary region, and the classical controller is never required to plan over a long horizon.}

\rev{The hybrid controller governs motion once the path is underway. However, the achievable path-following length is also fixed before the first control step, by the configuration in which the arm begins. The inverse-kinematics preimage of the initial constraint is a one-dimensional self-motion manifold, so a poor choice of starting branch can commit the arm to an early joint-limit encounter, regardless of how the redundancy is subsequently resolved. To address this, we introduce a conditional diffusion model that, conditioned on the task specification, samples initial configurations from branches that are robust to task perturbations. The two components are complementary in time: the diffusion model selects a favorable starting branch at $t=0$, while the hybrid controller sustains motion along it thereafter.}


\rev{This paper makes the following contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item A step-level hybrid null-space controller that arbitrates between an RL policy and a classical controller through a hysteresis rule keyed on the normalized joint-limit proximity, together with a length-maximizing RL formulation that uses a progress-only reward and no terminal penalty so that survival is the sole return signal.
\item As an enabling component, a conditional diffusion model trained on self-motion-manifold labels initializes the arm on a robust starting branch, providing the hybrid controller with a sound starting configuration; this initializer is not claimed to be a general-purpose seed selector, only to outperform hand-designed heuristics on the present task family.
\item A factorial seed-by-controller evaluation protocol on $10{,}000$ stratified straight-line tasks for a 7-DoF FR3, reported through length ratios against a reference length rather than absolute success rates, which isolates the contribution of each component.
\end{itemize}}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Inverse Kinematics Redundancy Resolution}

\rev{Inverse-kinematics (IK) methods fall into analytical, numerical, and learning-based families; redundancy resolution is particularly critical for high-DoF systems.}

\rev{Analytical IK solvers transform the non-unique mapping into deterministic closed-form equations \cite{wu2021t}. Numerical approaches instead rely on iterative optimization to minimize the distance between the manipulator's current and target end-effector poses \cite{beeson2015trac}.} The core of numerical-IK methods for redundant manipulators is to utilize the null space in their Jacobian matrix to achieve secondary tasks, such as joint limit avoidance \cite{ananthanarayanan2015real} and task constraint satisfaction. For example, \cite{taghirad2011analytic} leveraged null space projection within an analytical-iterative framework to ensure cable tension for a parallel manipulator. To further mitigate singularities in the Jacobian matrix, \cite{wang2024hierarchical} proposed the Hierarchical Incremental Model Predictive Control (HIMPC) framework, where task priorities are maintained through equality constraints.

\rev{Redundancy stems from the one-to-many mapping between Cartesian task space and joint space. Unlike the explicit resolution of classical methods, this structure aligns naturally with learning-based methods, which capture the multi-modal distribution over the feasible solution space.} Prior work includes reinforcement learning-based methods \cite{malik2022deep} and generative approaches such as IKFlow \cite{ames2022ikflow} and IKDiffuser \cite{zhang2025ikdiffuser}.

Nevertheless, existing IK solvers primarily optimize local surrogate objectives. While preserving static feasibility, they do not explicitly consider long-horizon path-following execution. Consequently, identifying an appropriate candidate configuration that facilitates stable downstream motion remains challenging, particularly for task-space path-following with joint-space constraints. To address this issue, our proposed method explicitly integrates long-horizon motion expectation into the redundancy resolution process.

\subsection{Manipulability and Reachability}
Manipulability and reachability describe local instantaneous motion dexterity and global spatial feasibility, respectively.

Specifically, the manipulability ellipsoid \cite{yoshikawa1985manipulability} characterizes the relationship between joint and task-space velocities via the Jacobian. Building on this representation, directional capability can be evaluated by projecting the ellipsoid along a specified task-space direction. To utilize this measure online, \cite{jin2017manipulability} adopted a dynamic neural network to achieve inverse-free manipulability optimization. Recent studies have extended manipulability awareness to various motion planning structures. For example, \cite{shen2023adaptive} proposed a manipulability-aware $\text{RRT}^{\star}$ planner that integrated the measure into both the cost function and adaptive step size. Similarly, \cite{cai2025just} \rev{introduced} a motion performance module that balances path cost and manipulability during planning to mitigate singularities. To capture the dexterity of human experts, \cite{jaquier2021geometry} treated manipulability ellipsoids as geometric descriptors to learn from expert manipulability patterns.

Complementary to local dexterity, reachability analysis defines the global spatial boundaries. Early works by \cite{yang2009placement} and \cite{makhal2018reuleaux} established geometric and voxel-based reachability maps to automate base placement without exhaustive IK computations. To enhance task quality, \cite{yao2023enhanced} and \cite{chen2018dexterous} integrated whole-body dexterity and visual manipulability to optimize configurations in the \rev{null space} for dexterous grasping. Moving toward dynamic intelligence, \cite{feng2025predictive} introduced predictive reachability via world models for embodiment selection, while \cite{jauhri2022robot} utilized reachability priors to accelerate RL-based control policy learning. To bridge the gap between performance and safety, \cite{selim2022safe} proposed a Black-box Reachability-based Safety Layer (BRSL) using differentiable polytope collision checks, ensuring formal safety guarantees under unknown dynamics.

Despite these achievements, a critical gap remains between local instantaneous dexterity and global spatial feasibility. \rev{Owing to redundancy, the choice of configuration is critical for the subsequent motion, both for singularity avoidance and for maintaining reachability.} Current manipulability-related techniques effectively avoid local singularity but lack foresight, while reachability-based methods only provide static spatial priors.

\section{Preliminaries}
\subsection{Problem Formulation}
Consider a redundant manipulator with $n$ degrees of freedom, where $\mathbf{q} \in \mathbb{R}^{n}$ denotes the joint configuration. Let $\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{q}) \in \mathbb{R}^{3}$ denote the end-effector position. A straight-line path-following task is specified by the condition vector:
\begin{equation}
\label{cond}
\mathbf{c} = [\mathbf{p}_{0}; \mathbf{d}; \mathbf{n}] \in \mathbb{R}^{9},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{p}_{0}$ denotes the starting position, and $\mathbf{d}$ and $\mathbf{n}$ are unit vectors representing the desired motion direction and task-plane normal, respectively. 

The objective is to advance the end-effector along the prescribed line as far as possible. Starting from an initial configuration $\mathbf{q}_{0}^{*}$, the policy $\pi$ resolves the redundancy at every control step to maximize the path-following length, producing a joint trajectory $\{\mathbf{q}_{t}^{*}\}_{t=0}^{T}$ initialized at $\mathbf{q}_{0}^{*}$.

The above task can be formalized as a constrained optimization problem as:
\begin{align}
\max_{\mathbf{q}_{0}^{*},\,\pi} \quad & \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{q}^{*}_{0},\,\pi;\,\mathbf{p}_{0}, \mathbf{d}, \mathbf{n}) \\
\text{s.t.} \quad & \|\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{q}_{t}^{*}) - \mathbf{p}_{t}\| \leq \epsilon_{\text{pos}}, \\
& \arccos\!\left( \mathbf{z}(\mathbf{q}_{t}^{*})^{\top} \mathbf{n} \right) \leq \theta_{\max}, \label{eq:ori_constraint} \\
& \mathbf{q}_{\min} \preceq \mathbf{q}_{t}^{*} \preceq \mathbf{q}_{\max}, \label{eq:jl_constraint}
\end{align}
where $\mathcal{L}$ denotes the path-following length, $\mathbf{q}_0^*$ and $\pi$ are the initial configuration and the redundancy-resolution policy to be optimized, and $(\mathbf{p}_0, \mathbf{d}, \mathbf{n})$ specify the task conditions as above. The $\epsilon_{\text{pos}}$, $\theta_{\max}$, and $[\mathbf{q}_{\min}, \mathbf{q}_{\max}]$ are the position tolerance, orientation tolerance, and joint limits, respectively\footnote{The detailed thresholds can be found in the Appendix.}. All the constraints should be satisfied for the whole trajectory.

\subsection{Task Optimization via Null-Space Control}
\label{sec:baseline}
% here to state how is the joint velocity be decomposed, and how is the null space been improved via gradient asent. List the fomulars. the detailed formulas can be put in the appendix
For a redundant arm the position Jacobian $\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{q})=\partial\mathbf{p}/\partial\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times n}$ has a non-trivial null space, so the joint velocity that realizes a desired end-effector motion is not unique. The classical resolver decomposes the commanded joint velocity into a task term and a null-space term,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:nullspace}
\dot{\mathbf{q}} = \mathbf{J}^{+}\dot{\mathbf{x}} + \big(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{J}^{+}\mathbf{J}\big)\,\boldsymbol{\xi},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{J}^{+}=\mathbf{J}^{\top}(\mathbf{J}\mathbf{J}^{\top}+\lambda^{2}\mathbf{I})^{-1}$ is the damped pseudo-inverse with a Nakamura--Hanafusa adaptive damping $\lambda$ that grows only as the smallest singular value of $\mathbf{J}$ drops below a threshold, and $\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{J}^{+}\mathbf{J}$ projects the secondary command $\boldsymbol{\xi}$ onto the null space. The task velocity $\dot{\mathbf{x}}=v\,\mathbf{d}+\mathbf{K}_{P}(\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{ref}}-\mathbf{p})$ advances the end-effector along the line at speed $v$ while a proportional term rejects lateral drift from the reference point $\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{ref}}$ on the line. The secondary command performs gradient ascent on a weighted sum of redundancy objectives,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:secondary}
\boldsymbol{\xi}=k_{\mu}\,\nabla_{\mathbf{q}}w_{\mathbf{d}}(\mathbf{q})\;-\;k_{\mathrm{jl}}\,\frac{\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{mid}}}{\mathbf{q}_{\max}-\mathbf{q}_{\min}}\;+\;k_{\theta}\,g(\theta)\,\nabla_{\mathbf{q}}\!\big(\mathbf{z}(\mathbf{q})^{\top}\mathbf{n}\big),
\end{equation}
combining a directional-manipulability term $w_{\mathbf{d}}$ that retreats from singularities along $\mathbf{d}$, a centering term that pulls each joint toward the middle $\mathbf{q}_{\mathrm{mid}}$ of its range, and a cone term gated by $g(\theta)$ that activates only as the tool axis approaches the $\theta_{\max}$ boundary. The detailed weights and gradient expressions are given in the Appendix.

The two regimes are arbitrated at every control step by a hysteresis rule introduced below. \rev{A favorable starting branch cannot be obtained by sampling the inverse-kinematics preimage by density alone. For a fixed end-effector constraint this preimage is a one-dimensional self-motion manifold (SMM), and an SMM analysis shows that it generally splits into several \emph{disconnected} branches: configurations on different branches cannot be connected without violating the constraint, and they differ sharply in how far the subsequent path can be followed. The label-generation process is therefore designed for branch coverage rather than density. For each task $\mathbf{c}$ we (i) enumerate candidate configurations by cone-constrained IK, sampling tool orientations inside the $\theta_{\max}$ cone and solving damped IK from many seeds so that distinct branches are reached; (ii) walk the one-dimensional SMM around each candidate and group nearby solutions into branches; (iii) sample arc-length-uniform points along each branch so the manifold is covered evenly; (iv) score every sample by a clean rollout under the classical controller of Section~\ref{sec:baseline}, recording the achieved path-following length $\mathcal{L}$; and (v) re-evaluate the survivors under small task perturbations and retain only those whose length stays above a fixed fraction of its unperturbed value, so the kept labels are robust rather than brittle. The top-scoring robust configurations across all branches form the training labels, covering the distinct disconnected branches of the SMM. A full description of the SMM and the enumeration is given in the Appendix.}

\subsubsection{Training and Conditional-Guided Inference}\label{sec:dp_inference}
% introduce the training, condition inference (cfg)

% adjust the following paragraph into the real model we use
The proposed framework utilizes Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) \cite{ho2020denoising} with U-net and Transformer backbone. DDPM starts by adding Gaussian noise to the original data stepwise and uses the neural network to learn the inverse denoising process. During the forward process, Gaussian noise will be added gradually to the original data:
\begin{equation}
    q(\mathbf{x}_t|\mathbf{x}_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_t; \sqrt{1-\beta_t}\mathbf{x}_{t-1}, \beta_t\textbf{I}),
\end{equation}
where $\beta_t$ is the variance scheduler, $\mathbf{x}_t$ and $\mathbf{x}_{t-1}$ are samples from two adjacent diffusion steps.

During the reverse chain, the model starts from the pure Gaussian noise $\mathbf{x}_T$ and gradually extracts the noise to derive $\mathbf{x}_{T-1}$ ... $\mathbf{x}_0$. DDPM uses $p_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}|\mathbf{x}_{t})$ to approximate the conditional denoising distribution:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
p_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{t-1} \vert \mathbf{x}_t) = \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}; \boldsymbol{\mu}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t), \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t)).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\rev{In our instantiation the data $\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{q}_{0}$ is a $7$-dimensional joint configuration and the network is conditioned on the task vector $\mathbf{c}=[\mathbf{p}_{0};\mathbf{d};\mathbf{n}]$. The denoiser embeds $\mathbf{c}$ through a multilayer perceptron and the diffusion step through a sinusoidal embedding, adds them to the embedding of the noisy sample, and processes the sum with a stack of residual blocks; it is trained with the $\mathbf{v}$-prediction objective under a cosine noise schedule. To enable conditional guidance, the condition is replaced by a learnable null token $\varnothing$ with probability $p_{\mathrm{drop}}$ during training, so that a single network represents both the conditional and the unconditional score. At inference we sample with DDIM under classifier-free guidance,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cfg}
\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{v}_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t},t,\varnothing) + w\big(\mathbf{v}_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t},t,\mathbf{c}) - \mathbf{v}_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t},t,\varnothing)\big),
\end{equation}
where $w$ is the guidance scale: $w=1$ recovers conditional sampling and $w>1$ amplifies the task signal. At deployment we sample from the exponential-moving-average network in batches of $4$ and refine each candidate onto the start manifold---the exact $(\mathbf{p}_{0},\mathbf{n})$ pose---by Newton's method. Candidates are projected sequentially and the first IK-valid configuration is retained as the selected seed $\mathbf{q}_{0}^{*}$; if a batch contains no valid candidate, a fresh batch is drawn, up to a cap of $32$ attempts in total. In the rare event that all attempts fail we fall back to the task's generating configuration $\mathbf{q}_{0}^{\mathrm{seed}}$, which is always legal by construction. The procedure guarantees that $\mathbf{q}_{0}^{*}$ is legal but does not select among legal samples by downstream quality, keeping inference cost on the order of one Newton solve per task in the typical case.}


\subsection{RL-based Null-Space Controller}
\subsubsection{Policy}
% Proximal Policy Optimization
\rev{The interior policy $\pi$ is trained with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). The actor and critic are separate multilayer perceptrons that map the observation to, respectively, the mean of a diagonal Gaussian over the action and a scalar state value, with a learned state-independent action standard deviation. Training runs on a large batch of parallel line-following environments with generalized advantage estimation, advantage normalization, and the clipped surrogate objective; full hyperparameters are listed in the Appendix.}

\subsubsection{States}
\rev{The observation at each step is a $31$-dimensional vector that exposes only the quantities the policy needs in order to resolve redundancy. It concatenates the joint configuration normalized to $[-1,1]$ by the joint range and its element-wise square, so proximity to either limit is directly visible; the task direction $\mathbf{d}$ and plane normal $\mathbf{n}$; the current tool axis $\mathbf{z}(\mathbf{q})$ together with its alignment $\mathbf{z}(\mathbf{q})^{\top}\mathbf{n}$ and the cross product $\mathbf{z}(\mathbf{q})\times\mathbf{n}$, which encode the orientation error relative to the cone; and the previous action. The end-effector position is deliberately excluded, because lateral tracking along the line is handled by the controller rather than the policy.}

\subsubsection{Actions}
% Task-Aligned Null-Space Basis Construction
\rev{Rather than command joint velocities directly, the policy outputs a $4$-dimensional action $\mathbf{a}\in[-1,1]^{4}$ whose entries are coordinates in a task-aligned null-space basis. At each step we construct an orthonormal basis $\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{q})\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times 4}$ of the position null space by modified Gram--Schmidt on the projected gradients of the redundancy objectives---directional manipulability, cone alignment, and joint-limit centering---completed by the remaining null direction, with an SVD fallback when a gradient is degenerate. The null-space command is $\dot{\mathbf{q}}_{\mathrm{null}}=\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{q})\,(a_{\max}\mathbf{a})$, where $a_{\max}$ scales the action to a joint-velocity magnitude, and it is added to the same task term used by the classical resolver in Eq.~\eqref{eq:nullspace}. Aligning the action with interpretable redundancy directions keeps the search compact---$4$ dimensions instead of $n$---and shares the same parameterization as the classical controller, which makes the two regimes directly interchangeable at switching time.}

\subsubsection{Rewards}
\rev{Consistent with the objective of maximizing the path-following length, the reward is progress-only:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:reward}
r_{t} = w_{p}\,\mathrm{clip}\!\left(\frac{(\mathbf{p}_{t+1}-\mathbf{p}_{t})^{\top}\mathbf{d}}{v\,\Delta t},\,0,\,1\right)\in[0,\,w_{p}],
\end{equation}
that is, the per-step end-effector advance along $\mathbf{d}$ normalized by its maximum attainable value. No terminal penalty is applied: a collision, a cone violation, or a joint-limit violation simply ends the episode with no further reward. Because every surviving step contributes almost equally and termination only stops the accrual, the return is monotonically tied to the survival time, so the policy is rewarded purely for sustaining the motion rather than for optimizing any instantaneous surrogate.}

\subsection{Null-Space Policies Coordination}\label{sec:coordination}
% how do we devide the whole configuration space
% how to switch
\rev{The two controllers are combined at the level of individual control steps. We measure proximity to the joint-limit boundary by the normalized distance
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rho}
\rho(\mathbf{q}) = \max_{i}\,\frac{|q_{i}-q_{\mathrm{mid},i}|}{(q_{\max,i}-q_{\min,i})/2}\in[0,1],
\end{equation}
which is $0$ at the center of the joint range and $1$ at a limit. The interior $\{\rho<\tau\}$ is assigned to the RL policy and the near-boundary shell $\{\rho\ge\tau\}$ to the classical controller. To suppress chattering when $\rho$ hovers near the threshold, switching follows a hysteresis rule with two thresholds $\tau_{\mathrm{enter}}\ge\tau_{\mathrm{exit}}$: while the RL policy is active it is retained until $\rho\ge\tau_{\mathrm{enter}}$, at which point control passes to the classical controller; once classical, control returns to the RL policy only after $\rho$ falls back below $\tau_{\mathrm{exit}}$. An episode whose seed already lies inside the boundary shell, $\rho(\mathbf{q}_{0}^{*})\ge\tau_{\mathrm{enter}}$, begins under the classical controller. In this way the RL policy never acts in the under-sampled boundary region, and the classical controller is never required to plan over a long horizon.}

\section{Experiments and Analysis}
\rev{\label{sec:exp}}
All experiments were conducted using the One robotic system\footnote{https://github.com/wanweiwei07/one}, on the 7-DoF Franka Research~3 (FR3) with a pen-shaped tool attached to the end-effector. The evaluation set comprises $10{,}000$ tasks, each specified by a starting position $\mathbf{p}_0$, a desired line direction $\mathbf{d}$, and a target work surface normal $\mathbf{n}$, sampled within the FR3 work range from the same task population used to train the diffusion model.

Unless otherwise specified, experiments use the default setting: (1) a diffusion-based initial configuration generator using DDIM with 50 denoising steps and CFG scale $w=1.5$; (2) each generated sample seeds the downstream SelIK solver, retaining the first valid one within at most 32 trials; (3) hybrid switching thresholds $\tau_{\mathrm{enter}},\tau_{\mathrm{exit}}$ of $0.98,0.94$; (4) classical secondary-objective weights $k_{\mu},k_{\mathrm{jl}},k_{\theta}$ of $0.8,0.4,0.2$.

For each evaluation task, we generate seed candidates following the procedure of Section~4.1 (up to $6$ seeds per task) and roll out every candidate under the deployed hybrid controller. We denote the longest rollout length as the \emph{reference length} ($\ell^{\mathrm{ref}}$) and utilize it for subsequent percentage comparison.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\rev{We presented a joint-limit-aware redundancy-resolution framework for straight-line Cartesian path following. Its core is a step-level hybrid null-space controller that, guided by a hysteresis rule on the normalized joint-limit distance $\rho(\mathbf{q})$, assigns the well-sampled interior to a length-maximizing reinforcement-learning policy and delegates the boundary shell to a certified classical controller. A conditional diffusion model serves as an enabling component, initializing the arm on a robust branch of the self-motion manifold at $t{=}0$. On $10{,}000$ held-out straight-line tasks for a 7-DoF FR3 the hybrid system extends the path-following length by $1.26\times$ over the classical baseline and recovers $90.6\%$ of a controller-aware reference, with the boundary-aware partitioning supported by the controller-wise inversion across difficulty buckets in Table~\ref{tab:lnetresult}: RL outperforms classical control on Easy and Medium tasks (interior-dominated trajectories) but loses to it on Difficult tasks (boundary-dominated trajectories), and the hybrid inherits the better of the two in every bucket. The design is further validated by the post-hoc termination breakdown over the $10{,}000$-task evaluation: joint-limit overshoot, the failure mode the hybrid coordination is designed to suppress, accounts for only $11.6\%$ of terminations, with cone violation ($65.9\%$), lateral drift ($19.8\%$), and self-collision ($2.8\%$) making up the rest.}

\rev{Two limitations remain. First, tool-orientation cone violation is the dominant residual failure mode; the classical cone-term gain $k_{\theta}$ is inert on this task distribution (Section~\ref{sec:ablate}), so neither sub-controller actively tracks orientation while the tool axis is still inside the cone. Integrating an active cone-tracking term---rather than the boundary-gated $g(\theta)$ used here---into either the classical or the RL action would directly address this. Second, the deployment-time IK fallback affects roughly $4\%$ of tasks and is responsible for a sizeable share of the Easy-bucket gap to the reference ($17.6$\,pp, Table~\ref{tab:lnetresult}), because the seed-selection policy returns the first IK-valid sample without searching for the longest-rollout one whereas the reference is computed over the entire candidate pool; a closed-loop seed generator that conditions on the failure of the previous Newton step is a natural fix. We also leave to future work the transfer to other line-following primitives and to different kinematic chains.}
