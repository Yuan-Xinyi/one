Generation: KDTree-Seeded Candidate Enumeration and Closed-Loop Return
Supervision}

\begin{abstract}
This paper studies the initialization of extreme motion generation, in
which a redundant manipulator follows a prescribed straight-line path for
as long as possible before a joint limit or a tool-orientation tolerance
forces termination. The achievable path length is largely decided before
the motion begins, by the choice of the starting configuration among the
disconnected branches of the cone-constrained inverse-kinematics solution
set, yet the consequence of this choice is revealed only deep into the
closed-loop execution. The proposed method extends our KDTree-based seed
selection solver to this long-horizon setting: the configuration space is
discretized by Centroidal Voronoi Tessellation sampling and indexed in a
KDTree; tool directions sampled inside the orientation cone query the
tree; candidates are ranked by minimal joint-space adjustment through the
stored Jacobian pseudo-inverses and refined by damped least-squares
iterations with a farthest-from-tried re-attempt strategy; and a
listwise-trained ensemble selector, supervised by the complete
closed-loop return of every feasible candidate, commits to a single
starting configuration that a hybrid null-space controller then executes
in one rollout. Experiments on \nSealed-scale path-following tasks with a
7-DoF manipulator show that the table-based candidate layer attains the
attainable ceiling of exhaustive random-restart enumeration at roughly
one third of its solving budget, that closed-loop return supervision is
necessary---every instantaneous geometric criterion underperforms a
trivial first-feasible rule---and that the recovered fraction of the
attainable headroom is bounded by a measured limit of execution-free
selection. Detailed ablation studies analyze the table construction, the
re-attempt strategy, the supervision signal, and the selector capacity,
providing practical insights for customization.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Extreme motion generation, inverse kinematics, seed selection, redundant
manipulators, reinforcement learning.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section*{Note to Practitioners}
The proposed pipeline is implemented in the \texttt{Yuan/unified\_rl}
package of our repository. The candidate layer is
\texttt{iksel\_campaign.py} (stage \texttt{gen}); the table is built once
by \texttt{iksel\_clean\_pilot.py} (stage \texttt{table2}) and is robot-
and task-independent, so practitioners can reuse it across applications
by rebuilding it for their own kinematic chain (minutes on one GPU).
Three parameters matter most in customization: the number of cone
directions per task (\texttt{N\_DIRS}, default 32) trades candidate
diversity against generation time; the maximum re-attempts per direction
(\texttt{MAX\_TRY}, default 4) governs the solve rate near difficult
branches; and the positional weight of the KDTree key (default
$1/0.05\,\mathrm{m}$) encodes how strongly retrieval prioritizes
workspace proximity over tool-axis alignment. The selector is trained by
stage \texttt{train-selector} from complete rollout tables and is the
only learned component; systems without a learned controller can replace
the execution stage by any tracking controller and retrain the selector
on its returns, as the selection machinery makes no assumption about the
controller beyond the ability to roll out candidates offline.

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{C}{onsider} a fixed-base redundant manipulator coating or
welding a workpiece along a straight line. The practical stroke of such
an operation terminates long before the boundary of the reachable
workspace: one joint reaches its limit, or the tool axis drifts out of
its orientation tolerance. We refer to the problem of maximizing the
constrained Cartesian path length along a prescribed trajectory as
\textit{extreme motion generation}. Its industrial value is direct,
because the length of one continuous motion determines the area a
manipulator can process in a single setup.

For a task that constrains fewer degrees of freedom than the arm
possesses, the feasible starting configurations form a self-motion
manifold whose joint-limit-induced branches are disconnected.
Configurations on different branches realize the same tool pose yet
support very different downstream path lengths: on our task
distribution, the best and the worst feasible start of the same task
differ by a median of 472\,mm of final path length, comparable to the
average task length itself. The initialization problem is therefore
decisive. It is also deceptively hard, for two reasons that
Sec.~\ref{sec:exp} quantifies. First, the consequence of the choice is
revealed late: whether a branch survives is dominated by
orientation-cone violations that occur deep into the trajectory, and
correlates only weakly with any instantaneous geometric quantity
evaluated at the start---selection rules built on manipulability or
joint-limit clearance perform \textit{worse} than taking the first
feasible candidate. Second, the decision must be made without trial: an
execution budget of one rollout per task excludes probing, and because a
complete episode spans only a few tens of control steps, an informative
probe would cost nearly as much as executing every candidate.

In our previous work we developed IKSel~\cite{yuan2026iksel}, a seed
selection method that discretizes the configuration space by Centroidal
Voronoi Tessellation (CVT), indexes it in a KDTree, ranks candidate
configurations by minimal joint-space adjustment, and re-attempts with
farther seeds upon failure. IKSel answers the question ``from which seed
will a numerical iteration converge \textit{now}''; the present paper
extends the same machinery to answer ``from which configuration will a
closed-loop execution survive \textit{longest}.'' The extension is
non-trivial precisely because the second question cannot be answered by
any instantaneous criterion, as our experiments demonstrate.

The proposed pipeline operates as follows. Offline, the configuration
space is discretized once by CVT sampling and stored with forward
kinematics and Jacobian pseudo-inverses in a KDTree; the table is
task-independent and contains no learned component. Online, tool
directions sampled inside the orientation cone form full query poses;
each query retrieves nearest neighbors, ranks them by the joint-space
adjustment metric of IKSel, and refines the top candidate by damped
least-squares iterations, re-attempting with the candidate farthest from
previously tried seeds upon failure. The surviving solutions,
deduplicated in joint space, constitute a compact candidate set that
spans the self-motion branches. A permutation-equivariant ensemble
selector---trained offline on the \textit{complete closed-loop return}
of every feasible candidate, i.e., the path length actually achieved
when the controller runs that candidate to termination---scores the set
once and commits to a single configuration, from which a hybrid
null-space controller executes the motion. The deployed budget is one
table query, one selector inference, one seed, and one rollout.

Contributions are threefold. First, we show that the table-based
candidate layer attains the attainable ceiling of exhaustive
random-restart enumeration at roughly one third of its solving budget,
and we trace, through single-variable ablations, exactly which
ingredients this requires: CVT \textit{sampling} rather than clustered
compression of the table, and the re-attempt strategy rather than table
density. Second, we establish that closed-loop return supervision is
necessary for the selection stage: on the identical candidate set and
feature set, every instantaneous criterion captures a negative fraction
of the attainable headroom, whereas return supervision captures roughly
two thirds. Third, we locate the limit of execution-free selection:
capacity, schedule, and even an exact kinematic continuation of every
candidate leave the captured fraction unchanged, indicating that the
residual headroom depends on the learned controller's closed-loop
behavior and is invisible before execution. All findings are validated
under a preregistered protocol with a sealed final evaluation.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Seed Selection and Generative Inverse Kinematics}
Numerical IK methods remain sensitive to their initial seed, and seed
selection techniques have been studied to accelerate
convergence~\cite{yuan2026iksel}. Learning-based solvers model the
multi-modal solution set directly, with normalizing
flows~\cite{ames2022ikflow}, diffusion models~\cite{zhang2025ikdiffuser},
and trajectory-level seeding~\cite{huang2024diffusionseeder}. These
methods address the question of reaching a pose; the present work
addresses the question of \textit{which} of the many reachable
configurations sustains the longest subsequent motion, and shows that
the answer requires supervision that no pose-level criterion provides.

\subsection{Redundancy Resolution and Hybrid Control}
Classical redundancy resolution optimizes instantaneous objectives such
as manipulability~\cite{yoshikawa1985manipulability} in the null space
of the task Jacobian, and joint-limit handling during iteration has been
addressed by weighted and clamping
schemes~\cite{huang2016clamping,sekiguchi2021numerical}. Learned
policies supply long-horizon foresight but degrade near the
sparsely-sampled joint-limit boundary; residual and switching
architectures combine the two
paradigms~\cite{johannink2019residual,alshiekh2018safe,kurtz2021control}.
Our execution stage adopts a switching design gated by joint-limit
proximity with hysteresis, and our experiments additionally verify a
property that simplifies the overall system: under task-relative
observations and randomized-task training, the execution stage requires
no adaptation to the initialization-induced state distribution.

\subsection{Sequential Policy Chaining}
When a system decomposes into sequential stages, the output distribution
of one stage becomes the input distribution of the next, and
bidirectional fine-tuning has been proposed to align adjacent
policies~\cite{chen2023sequential}. We adopt the backward direction of
this credit structure---initialization is evaluated by the success of
execution---and treat the necessity of the forward direction as an
experimental question, answered negatively for this system with a
stratified analysis that explains the mechanism.

\section{Candidate Enumeration Using KDTree}
\label{sec:candidate}
The convergence of a numerical iteration and the survival of a
closed-loop execution are both highly sensitive to the starting
configuration. This section extends the KDTree framework of
IKSel~\cite{yuan2026iksel} from the former problem to the latter: the
table supplies workspace-proximal, adjustment-ranked seeds; cone-directed
queries and re-attempted refinement turn them into a branch-spanning
candidate set.

\subsection{CVT-Based Sampling and KDTree Construction}
\label{sec:cvt}
The construction follows~\cite{yuan2026iksel}. The joint space
$\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{7}$ is discretized by CVT sampling into
$k{=}201{,}600$ generators $\{\mathbf{z}_i\}$, each coinciding with the
mass centroid of its Voronoi cell under a uniform density; in practice
the generators are obtained by Lloyd iterations on a large uniform
sample pool, so that every generator is a genuine, minimally-averaged
configuration.\footnote{This distinction matters. In
Sec.~\ref{sec:abl-table} we show that compressing the same pool into few
thousand k-means centroids---averages over large cells---degrades the
attainable ceiling by more than 20\,mm, whereas full-scale CVT sampling
does not.} For each $\mathbf{q}_i$, forward kinematics yields the tool
center position $\mathbf{p}_i$ and tool axis $\mathbf{z}(\mathbf{q}_i)$,
and the full Jacobian pseudo-inverse $\mathbf{J}^{+}_i$ is precomputed by
singular value decomposition with regularization. The KDTree key is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{x}_i=\big[\,\mathbf{p}_i/w_{\mathrm{pos}},\;
\mathbf{z}(\mathbf{q}_i)\,\big],\qquad w_{\mathrm{pos}}=0.05\,
\mathrm{m},
\label{eq:key}
\end{equation}
where the positional scale equates a 5\,cm workspace deviation with
roughly one radian of tool-axis deviation. Representing the rotational
component by the tool axis rather than a full rotation vector reflects
the axial symmetry of the tool: rotation about the tool axis is
unconstrained by the task and should not contribute to the
metric.\footnote{For tools without axial symmetry the rotation-vector
key of~\cite{yuan2026iksel} applies unchanged.}

\subsection{Cone-Directed Query and Joint-Adjustment Ranking}
\label{sec:query}
A task is specified by $\mathbf{c}=[\mathbf{p}_0,\mathbf{d},\mathbf{n}]$:
the start position, the motion direction, and the nominal tool axis with
tolerance cone half-angle $\theta_{\max}{=}30^\circ$. Because any tool
axis inside the cone is admissible, the query stage samples $m{=}32$
directions $\{\mathbf{n}_j\}$ uniformly on the spherical cap (the first
being $\mathbf{n}$ itself), and each direction forms a full query pose
$(\mathbf{p}_0,\mathbf{n}_j)$. For each query, the $200$ nearest table
entries are retrieved and ranked by the approximated joint-space
adjustment
\begin{equation}
\Delta\mathbf{q}_i=\mathbf{J}^{+}_i\!
\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{p}_0-\mathbf{p}_i\\[1pt]
\boldsymbol{\rho}(\mathbf{z}(\mathbf{q}_i),\mathbf{n}_j)\end{bmatrix},
\label{eq:rank}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\rho}(\cdot,\cdot)$ is the axis-angle vector of the
minimal rotation aligning the stored tool axis with the queried
direction. As in~\cite{yuan2026iksel}, candidates requiring smaller
$\|\Delta\mathbf{q}_i\|$ are prioritized: small adjustments indicate that
the seed lies in a locally well-behaved region of the kinematic function,
which increases the likelihood of rapid convergence without scaling
coefficients.

\subsection{Refinement and Re-Attempt}
\label{sec:retry}
The top-ranked seed of each direction is refined by damped least-squares
iterations toward its query pose. If the iteration fails to produce a
strictly feasible solution---joint limits with margin, 5\,mm position
error, the orientation cone, and collision freedom---the re-attempt
strategy of~\cite{yuan2026iksel} is applied: the remaining candidates of
that direction are re-sorted in descending distance from the mean of the
previously tried seeds, and the next candidate is attempted, up to four
attempts per direction. The re-sorting drives consecutive attempts into
distinct regions of the configuration space, reducing the likelihood of
repeated failures caused by the same joint-limit constraint.
Sec.~\ref{sec:abl-retry} shows that this single mechanism raises the
per-direction solve rate from 72.9\,\% to 87.7\,\% and accounts for most
of the ceiling recovered over a no-re-attempt design. Finally, the
surviving solutions of all directions are deduplicated at $0.08$\,rad in
joint space, and a deterministic classical fallback configuration is
appended as a designated final slot, yielding a candidate set
$Q(\mathbf{c})$ with a median of 29 entries.

\section{Return-Supervised Selection and Hybrid Execution}
\label{sec:selection}

\subsection{Why Instantaneous Criteria Fail}
A natural selection rule scores each candidate by an instantaneous
quantity---manipulability along the motion direction, clearance from
joint limits, or the linearized travel horizon. Sec.~\ref{sec:abl-sup}
shows that on the enumerated candidate set every such rule captures a
\textit{negative} fraction of the attainable headroom, i.e., performs
worse than taking the first feasible candidate. The reason is structural
rather than a matter of tuning: branch survival is dominated by
orientation-cone violations that occur after tens of control steps, at
configurations far from the start, where the accumulated null-space
motion---not the initial geometry---determines feasibility. The
selection stage therefore requires supervision that observes the end of
the motion.

\subsection{Complete-Return Labeling}
With the execution controller frozen, every feasible candidate of every
training task is rolled out to termination, producing a complete
per-candidate return table $\mathcal{L}_{jk}$: the net path length
achieved from candidate $k$ of task $j$. Complete tables, rather than
sampled interactions, make the supervision exhaustive over the discrete
action set; the residual error of a selector trained on them measures
what its input features cannot express, a property exploited in
Sec.~\ref{sec:abl-limit}.

\subsection{Listwise Ensemble Selector}
Each candidate is described by 45 features: the controller's initial
observation at that configuration, the lateral ray error, log positional
manipulability, and ten task-aligned differential-kinematics descriptors
(the damped line-tracking joint velocity and its norm, a linearized
joint-limit travel horizon, and directional manipulability). The
selector is an ensemble of five permutation-equivariant members; each
member embeds the candidates, mean-pools a set context, concatenates it
back to every candidate, and outputs a listwise score head trained by
softmax cross-entropy on within-task range-normalized returns, together
with a feasibility head regressing the return in metres under a Huber
loss. Members are trained on task-level bootstrap resamples and
aggregated by mean score; deployment is the masked $\arg\max$ over
feasible candidates.

\subsection{Hybrid Null-Space Execution}
The execution stage resolves the redundancy at every control step
through the standard decomposition
$\dot{\mathbf{q}}=\mathbf{J}^{+}\dot{\mathbf{x}}
+(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{J}^{+}\mathbf{J})\boldsymbol{\xi}$, where the task
term tracks the advancing reference and the secondary command
$\boldsymbol{\xi}$ is produced by one of two interchangeable regimes: a
PPO-trained policy with task-relative observations in the interior of
the configuration space, and a classical gradient controller combining
directional manipulability, joint centering, and a gated cone term near
the joint-limit boundary. The active regime is chosen by the normalized
joint-limit proximity with a hysteresis pair
$(\tau_{\mathrm{enter}},\tau_{\mathrm{exit}})=(0.985,0.96)$ to suppress
chattering. The policy is trained on $10^{5}$ randomized tasks with
mixed reset distributions; Sec.~\ref{sec:abl-fwd} verifies that no
adaptation of this stage to the selection-induced distribution is
required, and explains why.

\section{Experiments and Analysis}
\label{sec:exp}
We first conduct ablation studies analyzing each component of the
candidate layer and the selection stage, and subsequently perform the
system-level evaluation under a preregistered protocol. All experiments
were conducted on a PC with a 13th Gen Intel Core i7 CPU, an NVIDIA
GeForce RTX-4090 GPU, and 64 GB of RAM, running Ubuntu. The manipulator
is a simulated 7-DoF Franka FR3 carrying an axially-symmetric tool.
Tasks are straight-line path-following queries drawn from a pool of
$10^{5}$ random lines filtered for feasibility; the objective is the net
path length before any constraint violation. The dataset splits are:

\begin{itemize}
\item[$\triangleright$] \underline{Training}: \nTrain{} tasks with
complete candidate return tables ($\approx$\ph{5.4}$\times10^{5}$
episodes);
\item[$\triangleright$] \underline{Validation / External}: two
development sets of \nDev{} tasks each, the latter built from a freshly
seeded pool with audited zero geometry overlap;
\item[$\triangleright$] \underline{Sealed}: \nSealed{} tasks generated
from fresh seeds after all weights and rules were frozen and
preregistered; read once by the published selector.
\end{itemize}

Paired per-task statistics are reported with a
$2{\times}10^{4}$-resample bootstrap 95\,\% confidence interval, the
5\,\% two-sided trimmed mean, and $>$1\,mm win/regress fractions.
Selection quality within a pool is summarized by the captured fraction
of the oracle headroom,
$(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sel}}-\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{first}})/
 (\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{oracle}}-\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{first}})$, where
the oracle executes every feasible candidate and is a diagnostic, not a
method.

\subsubsection{Efficacy of the Re-Attempt Strategy}
\label{sec:abl-retry}
Holding the full-scale table fixed, disabling re-attempts (a single
refinement per direction) yields a 72.9\,\% per-direction solve rate and
a median of 23 feasible candidates; enabling the farthest-from-tried
re-attempt with at most four attempts raises these to 87.7\,\% and 29.
The mechanism mirrors its role in numerical IK~\cite{yuan2026iksel}:
failures concentrate on directions whose nearest branch is blocked by a
joint limit, and re-sorting by distance from tried seeds moves the next
attempt into a different branch rather than repeating the same failure.
The ceiling gain attributable to this single mechanism is
$+$\ph{14}\,mm, the largest of any component in the candidate layer.

\subsubsection{Candidate-Set Size}
The attainable ceiling saturates once the self-motion branches are
covered: under farthest-point ordering of the enumeration reference, the
ceiling at $K{=}16$ already reaches 98.4\,\% of its $K{=}32$ value, and
the marginal contribution beyond $K{\approx}24$ falls below measurement
noise. The proposed layer's 32 cone directions with deduplication land
in this saturated regime; enlarging $m$ mainly adds duplicates.

\subsubsection{The Limit of Execution-Free Selection}
\label{sec:abl-limit}
The captured fraction plateaus near two thirds, and the plateau is a
property of the information rather than of the model: widening the
network, doubling the training schedule, and exchanging the encoder all
leave held-out capture within noise of the base configuration. As the
decisive control, we grant the selector the strongest execution-free
information available---for every candidate, the damped tracking field
is integrated on the pure kinematic model until a constraint is met,
yielding an exact nonlinear continuation of the starting branch with no
environment step and no policy call. This kinematic continuation is
individually excellent (within-task rank correlation 0.55 with the true
outcome, matching the entire trained selector at 0.53), yet appended to
the features and retrained it changes the captured fraction by nothing.
We conclude that the learned selector had already internalized the
kinematic continuation from its one-step descriptors, and that the
residual third of the headroom depends on the learned interior policy's
closed-loop behavior---how it spends the null space in ways no fixed
field anticipates---which is observable only by execution. This bounds
every execution-free selection scheme on this problem, the proposed one
included.

\subsubsection{Does the Execution Stage Require Adaptation?}
\label{sec:abl-fwd}
Sequential-chaining practice suggests adapting the controller to the
initialization-induced state
distribution~\cite{chen2023sequential}. We tested this direction twice
under a preregistered promotion gate: a naive continuation of policy
training on the selection-induced resets, and a conservatively
engineered round with critic-only warm-up, no entropy bonus, a tightened
trust region, and a fourfold step budget. Neither produced a significant
improvement on either development set, the first being significantly
harmful, and the training reward remained saturated throughout. A
stratified analysis explains the null result: although the selector
chooses configurations far from anything a generative initialization
proposes (median joint-space novelty above 2\,rad), the adaptation
effect is null in every novelty quartile, and the unadapted controller
performs best from the most novel branches. We attribute this to the
combination of task-relative observations and randomized-task training:
in the state space the policy actually observes, an unfamiliar branch of
one task coincides with familiar states of others, so the distribution
shift that adaptation would repair has been absorbed by design. The same
asymmetry---backward supervision effective, forward adaptation
null---was reproduced independently on two further candidate mechanisms
in our development history, suggesting a property of the observation
design rather than an accident of one system.

\section{Conclusions and Future Work}
We extended KDTree-based seed selection from numerical IK convergence to
long-horizon motion survival. The proposed candidate layer---CVT
sampling, cone-directed queries, joint-adjustment ranking, and
re-attempted refinement---attains the ceiling of exhaustive enumeration
at a third of its solving budget while remaining task-independent and
free of learned components; the selection stage requires, and is
sufficient with, complete closed-loop return supervision; and the
captured fraction of the attainable headroom is bounded by a measured
limit of execution-free selection, attributable to the closed-loop
behavior of the learned interior policy. Since the framework treats the
execution stage as a black box that can roll out candidates offline, it
can naturally incorporate other controllers and other long-horizon
objectives. Extending the selection stage with risk-sensitive objectives
that penalize per-task regressions, and validating the coverage argument
of Sec.~\ref{sec:abl-fwd} on physical hardware, are directions we are
interested in exploring.

\appendices

\section{Negative Results on Selection Augmentation}
\label{app:negative}
For completeness we document selection variants that did not improve the
proposed method, following the practice of reporting the boundary of a
design space rather than only its optimum.

\subsubsection*{Donor-Label Priors}
When the table stores, for each entry, the return it achieved in the
task from which it originated, retrieval could in principle supply a
nonparametric outcome prior. Measured on the validation set, the
within-task rank correlation between donor returns and the actual
returns of the refined candidates is only $0.089$, and selecting by
donor return captures $7.6\,\%$ of the headroom. Returns are a joint
function of configuration and task geometry and do not transfer at
neighbor granularity; the finding independently reconfirms the
execution-free limit of Sec.~\ref{sec:abl-limit}.

\subsubsection*{Abstention Gates}
A margin gate that falls back to the classical configuration when the
selector's feasibility margin is small \textit{raised} the regression
fraction from 37\,\% to 50\,\% on the development sets. The enumerated
candidate set intentionally contains no strong anchor---its
first-feasible reference is weak---so abstention substitutes a worse
default for a merely uncertain choice. Safe abstention requires an
anchor at least as strong as the baseline being protected, which this
design philosophy deliberately omits.

\subsubsection*{Compressed Tables at Increased Scale}
Increasing the compressed table from 2{,}048 to 16{,}384 centroids
changes no measured quantity (Table~\ref{tab:table-abl}), confirming
that centroid averaging, not resolution, is the failure mode of
compression.
